Richest Man In Babylon

The setting is ancient Babylon. Arkad, the richest man there, teaches people how he got rich.

The 7 Cures for a Lean Purse (a “lean purse” means having no money):

  1. Start your purse to fattening. Save at least 1/10 of everything you earn, before spending.
  2. Control your spending. Know the difference between needs and wants. Live on the other 9/10.
  3. Make your money multiply. Put your savings somewhere it earns more money.
  4. Guard your money from loss. Only invest where your money is safe. Ask experts first.
  5. Make your home a profitable investment. Own your home instead of renting forever.
  6. Secure a future income. Plan for old age and for your family.
  7. Increase your ability to earn. Keep learning and improving your skills. This one is about you, not money.

The 5 Laws of Gold

In this chapter, Arkad’s son Nomasir is given a bag of gold. He loses it through bad deals, then learns these laws:

  1. Money comes to people who save at least 1/10.
  2. Money works hard for people who invest it well.
  3. Money stays with careful people who take advice from experts.
  4. Money leaves people who invest in things they don’t understand.
  5. Money runs from people chasing get-rich-quick schemes and tricksters.

My Reflection

I already practise Cure 7 by pursuing my degree at UTech, putting my best effort into my courses while working part-time and keeping up with exercise classes. These activities help me stay focused on my goals, maintain balance, and develop good habits. However, I struggle with Cure 2 because I sometimes buy clothes based on what I want in the moment and later regret spending the money. I could apply the 1/10 rule to my freelance web income or part-time earnings by saving 10% of what I earn instead of spending it impulsively. Over time, these savings could grow and help me invest in assets or build funds towards starting my own sportswear brand. Cure 7 also reminds me of the importance of developing my skills at UTech, where I can gain knowledge from my courses and use it to increase my earning potential through employment or freelance web development. I have also seen get-rich-quick schemes in Jamaica that promise people quick profits. Nomasir’s experience in the book, where he lost his gold after trusting tricksters who promised large returns, teaches me to research investment opportunities carefully and question promises that seem too good to be true. My biggest takeaway is the second law of gold, which teaches that money works hard for those who invest it wisely. This lesson encourages me to manage my money responsibly and develop better saving habits. Gordon “Butch” Stewart, the founder of Sandals Resorts, also inspires me as a Jamaican entrepreneur whose success motivates me to work towards building my own business.
